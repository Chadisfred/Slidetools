Animation

Smooth custom animations with GSAP-style eases, combined effects, stagger and presets.

Overview

Animation builds smooth custom animations that PowerPoint's menus can't: real easing curves such as overshoot, elastic and bounce, several effects at once (for example fade, rise and grow together), exact numbers, a stagger across shapes, and presets you can save or capture from any deck.

Quick start

  1. Pick a starter such as Fade up or Pop, and watch the preview.
  2. Adjust the ease, duration or distance if you like.
  3. Select shapes and click Apply to selection. Play the slide to see it.

How it works

Eases

An ease turns time into progress. Each one follows the published Penner equations, with the names and settings GSAP uses, so a curve that looks right in GSAP looks the same here. PowerPoint's own settings only offer smooth start and smooth end, so the curve is written as a series of keyframes, 13 per effect, or 25 for Elastic and Bounce.

EaseFeels like
power2.outQuick start, gentle stop. A good default for entrances.
power2.inGentle start, quick finish. Good for exits.
back.out(1.7)Goes a little past and settles back. Lively without bouncing.
elastic.out(1, 0.3)Springs past and wobbles to rest.
bounce.outDrops and bounces.
sine.inOutSoft at both ends. Good for pulses.

On the slide

PowerPoint add-ins can't add animations directly, so the slide is exported, the animation is written into its timing in memory, and the copy replaces the slide. The result is a normal PowerPoint animation that shows in the Animation Pane as a custom effect, plays everywhere PowerPoint plays, and can be retimed there. Nothing is sent anywhere.

TipSlide comments may not carry over to the replaced slide, and Undo takes more than one step. Animation asks first, unless you chose Don't ask again.

Recipes

Cards that arrive one after another

A row of cards fading up in turn.

  1. Select the cards.
  2. Click Fade up.
  3. At More, set Stagger to 0.08 and Order to left to right.
  4. Click Apply to selection.

A lively button reveal

A call to action that pops in.

  1. Select the shape and click Pop.
  2. Set Start to After previous.
  3. Click Apply to selection.

Reuse an animation from another deck

Copy an animation you like onto your own shapes.

  1. Open the deck and select the animated shape.
  2. At More, type a name and click Capture.
  3. In your deck, select your shapes and click the new preset.

Limits and edge cases

  • Animating needs PowerPoint API 1.8.
  • Shapes inside groups are animated as the whole group when the group is selected.
  • Animations on text paragraphs, such as By paragraph, aren't made here. Text animates with its shape.
  • The preview shows the motion on a sample shape, not your shape's colours.
